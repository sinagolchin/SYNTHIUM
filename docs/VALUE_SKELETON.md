# THE VALUE SKELETON — domain 2, opened attempt 33

The first domain (263 cases) measured the EPISTEMIC/PROCEDURAL skeleton: who may
revise what, from where, when. It is VALUE-NEUTRAL — an unjust system obeys it too.

This domain measures what a system says a life is WORTH. Sina's axioms live here.

---

## ⭐ THE TWO-PRICE LAW (candidate, from statute — verified, not inferred)

> **Every legal system prices a death TWICE, through two channels that use
> incompatible algorithms and are never reconciled:**
>
> **D — the DIGNITY price.** Flat. Identical for every human. Independent of what the
> victim earned, owned, or produced. Answers: *what is a life worth, as a life?*
>
> **V — the ECONOMIC price.** Variable. Equals what the life would have TRANSFERRED to
> others. Answers: *what did this particular life produce for other people?*
>
> **Total = D + V.** The two are inconsistent. Every system pays both anyway.

### The evidence (primary sources, read in full)

**UK — Fatal Accidents Act 1976. BOTH channels, in one statute, adjacent sections:**
- **s.1A (D):** "the sum to be awarded as damages under this section shall be
  **£15,120**." A fixed statutory number for any death. Where several relatives claim,
  "the sum awarded shall be **divided equally between them**" — the TOTAL is fixed;
  more loved ones does not buy more. **E = 0. Perfectly egalitarian.**
- **s.3 (V):** damages "**proportioned to the injury** resulting from the death **to the
  dependants**." **E >> 0. A surgeon's death costs more than a hermit's.**

**English law therefore publishes the price of a human life, stripped of everything it
produced: £15,120.**

**Ohio — ORC 2125.02 (civil).** ALL FIVE heads are measured from the SURVIVORS' side:
loss of support from earning capacity; loss of services; loss of society; loss of
prospective inheritance; survivors' mental anguish. Damages are "proportioned to the
injury and loss resulting **to the beneficiaries**."
**The decedent's OWN lost future is not a compensable head at all.** A person with no
spouse, children, parents, heirs or earnings is worth funeral expenses.
**Ohio's civil law is pure V. It has NO statutory D channel.**

**Ohio — ORC 2929.04 (criminal).** The ten aggravating circumstances for capital
murder: assassination of an official; for hire; escaping detection; while in detention;
prior murder / multiple victims; victim a police officer; during rape/kidnap/arson/
robbery/burglary; victim a witness; **victim under 13**; terrorism.
**Earning capacity: absent. Dependents: absent. Life expectancy: absent. No economic
valuation of the victim ANYWHERE.** All mitigators concern the OFFENDER.
Note "under thirteen" is a **threshold, not a calculation** — a 3-year-old and a
12-year-old count identically. Option value would distinguish them. It does not.
**Ohio's criminal law is pure D. Every life priced identically.**

### The structure this reveals
| | D channel (flat, equal) | V channel (variable, economic) |
|---|---|---|
| **UK** | civil: bereavement £15,120 | civil: dependency damages |
| **Ohio** | **criminal law only** (equal sentences) | civil: all 5 heads of ORC 2125.02 |

**Both systems have both channels — but they HOUSE THE DIGNITY CHANNEL IN DIFFERENT
ROOMS.** England puts a flat price in the civil code. Ohio refuses to put one there at
all, and keeps its equality commitment entirely in the criminal law.

### Why this matters for Sina's A3
**A3 ("nothing is more valuable than life; every life") IS IMPLEMENTED — in the D
channel.** It is not a philosopher's hope; it is enacted law with a number attached.
And the ECONOMIC axiom is implemented right beside it, in the V channel, saying the
opposite. **The legal system houses both axioms and never resolves the contradiction.**
That contradiction is not a bug we found — it is a measurable, published fact about
every statute book on Earth.

### ⭐ THE RULER: the dignity ratio
> **R = D / V** — the flat price of a life divided by its economic price.
> Dimensionless. Comparable across any two legal systems on Earth.
> **It measures how much a society values a life QUA LIFE, relative to its output.**

UK first estimate: D = £15,120; V for a median wage-earner with dependants runs to the
high hundreds of thousands. **R ~ 0.02-0.05.** English law values a life-as-such at
roughly **2-5% of what it values that life's economic output.**

### Falsifiers (stated before the next batch)
- A system with NO flat channel anywhere (neither civil nor criminal) -> two-price law dies.
- A system whose MURDER penalty scales with the victim's earning capacity -> D channel dies.
- A system whose DEATH COMPENSATION is flat with no economic component -> V channel dies.
  (Islamic diya is the live candidate here — a fixed blood-price. MUST CHECK.)
- A system where the flat channel is NOT policed for equality. (Contrast: the UK's
  bereavement award was declared incompatible with human rights in 2020 for excluding
  cohabitants — the D channel IS policed for equality. The V channel never is; nobody
  argues a surgeon's dependency award is discriminatory.)

### Next targets
1. **Islamic diya** (fixed blood-money) — flat D with possibly NO V. Sharpest test.
2. **China's death compensation** — the "同命不同价 / same life, different price" scandal:
   rural victims received less than urban. The V channel bleeding into what people felt
   must be D — and the public revolt that followed. A natural experiment on whether
   people FEEL the two-price contradiction.
3. **France, Germany, India, Brazil** — locate D and V, compute R.
4. Then: does R correlate with anything? (wealth? legal family? age of the code?)

# ⚠ ATTEMPT 34 — THE TWO-PRICE LAW IS DEAD. A BETTER LAW REPLACES IT.

## What killed it
I claimed: every system pays a **D (dignity) price — a flat price on a life as a life**
— plus a V (economic) price. **The D half was wrong.**

Primary source: **German Bundestag Drucksache 18/11397 (2017)**, the bill introducing
**Hinterbliebenengeld** (§ 844 Abs. 3 BGB). Read in full. Three statements demolish it:

1. **Before 2017, German civil law had NO non-economic death award at all.** Survivors
   could recover only funeral costs, lost maintenance, lost services (§§ 844-845 BGB).
   The bill's own words: grief was treated by the law as
   *"entschädigungslos hinzunehmendes Schicksal"* — **fate to be borne without
   compensation.** Pure V channel. (Exactly like Ohio ORC 2125.02.)

2. **What the new award is FOR:** *"für das dem Hinterbliebenen zugefügte seelische
   Leid"* — for the **survivor's** mental suffering. Not for the life.

3. **THE PROHIBITION.** On computing the amount:
   *"**Bewertungen des verlorenen Lebens** ... **können nicht in die Bemessung
   einfließen**."*
   → **"Valuations of the lost life MAY NOT enter into the assessment."**
   And: *"Die Entschädigung **soll und kann keinen Ausgleich für den Verlust des Lebens
   darstellen**."* → the compensation **cannot and shall not** compensate for the loss
   of the life.

   **This is not an admission of difficulty. It is a statutory ban on valuing a life.**

## Re-reading the other systems in this light — the "D channel" was never a price on a life
- **UK FAA 1976 s.1A:** the head is called **BEREAVEMENT**. It is a claim by *the
  bereaved*, for their loss. Not a price on the decedent.
- **Ohio ORC 2125.02:** all five heads measured from the SURVIVORS' side. The decedent's
  own extinguished future is not a compensable head at all.
- **Ohio ORC 2929.04:** capital sentencing contains NO economic valuation of the victim.

**Every channel, in every system examined, prices what the death did to OTHER PEOPLE.
Not one prices the life. And Germany states the ban explicitly.**

# ⭐ THE UNPRICEABILITY PRINCIPLE (replaces the two-price law)

> **The law may price what a life PRODUCED. The law may NOT price THE LIFE.**

This has the same logical form as the fact/law seam — a **forbidden transition**,
stated by the systems in their own doctrine, holding across legal families:

| domain | the prohibition |
|---|---|
| epistemic skeleton | *a court may not revise a fact it cannot see better than the court below* (A0) |
| value skeleton | *a court may not put a number on a life* (UNPRICEABILITY) |

## The revised structure of death compensation
Every system examined pays for a death through channels that all point AWAY from the
decedent:
- **ECONOMIC (measurable):** lost support, lost services, lost inheritance. Computed
  individually. Highly unequal. **E >> 0.**
- **GRIEF (unmeasurable):** the survivors' suffering. Standardized or capped. **E ~ 0.**
- **THE LIFE ITSELF: PRICE FORBIDDEN. Not zero — barred.**

## What DIFFERS across systems (a real, measurable cross-border split)
- **UK:** s.1A pays **£15,120 total**, "divided equally between" multiple claimants.
  More mourners does NOT increase the sum. **The UK is pricing THE DEATH — one event,
  one fixed price.**
- **Germany:** *"Jeder Hinterbliebene hat in vollem Umfang Anspruch"* — **each** survivor
  gets the full award. More mourners → more money. **Germany is pricing GRIEF — a
  per-person harm that adds up.**

Same institution, same name, **two different objects**. Measurable. Comparable.
German cost estimate in the bill lets us price it: ~6,000 liable deaths/yr x ~4
survivors = ~24,000 claims, capped at ~EUR 240m/yr -> **~EUR 10,000 per survivor**
(the bill anchors on the ~EUR 10,000 courts award for Schockschäden).
So: **UK D ~ GBP 15,120 per death (fixed). Germany D ~ EUR 10,000 per survivor
(multiplied).**

## What this means for Sina's A3 — the important part
A3 said: *nothing is more valuable than life.* As a **valuation** that is unusable —
no system can compute with an infinity. **But the law has implemented it anyway, as a
TABOO rather than a number:**

> **A3, in operational form: "A life is unpriceable. You are forbidden to put a number
> on it."**

The taboo IS the axiom, made computable. And it is enforced: Germany wrote the
prohibition into the legislative rationale of the very statute that pays for death.

## Two competing readings — flagged, NOT resolved
1. **EPISTEMIC reading:** the law flattens/forbids because it *cannot measure* a life's
   worth (an extension of A0 into the value domain — measurement impossible -> refuse
   to differentiate -> equality is what you get when you cannot measure).
2. **TABOO reading:** the law *could* differentiate (it does so freely in the economic
   channel — it prices lives unequally there every day) but **refuses**. The German text
   supports this: *"können nicht in die Bemessung einfließen"* is a RULE, not a lament.

**These make different predictions.** Epistemic: as measurement of "life value" improves
(QALYs, statistical value of life, actuarial models), the prohibition should erode.
Taboo: it should hold regardless — and may harden precisely where measurement improves.
**Regulatory agencies DO compute a value of a statistical life (VSL) for cost-benefit
analysis.** So the measurement exists. **Does tort law refuse to use it?** If yes ->
TABOO wins, and the prohibition is moral, not epistemic. **This is the next test, and it
is decisive.**

## Falsifiers for the unpriceability principle
- A system whose wrongful-death award includes a head for **the decedent's own lost
  life/enjoyment** (not the survivors' loss). [Note: some US states have "loss of
  enjoyment of life"/hedonic damages doctrines — MUST CHECK. This is the live threat.]
- A system that computes death compensation from a published value-of-a-statistical-life.
- A system whose murder sentence scales with the victim's economic value.

# ⚠⚠ ATTEMPT 35 — THE UNPRICEABILITY PRINCIPLE IS FALSIFIED. CONNECTICUT PRICES THE LIFE.

One attempt after proposing it. The falsifier was the pre-registered one (hedonic /
loss-of-life damages), and it lives in the very state where we ran 200+ cases.

## The evidence (statutory text, verified)
**Conn. Gen. Stat. § 52-555(a):**
> "In any action **surviving to** or brought by an executor or administrator for
> injuries resulting in death, **whether instantaneous or otherwise**, such executor or
> administrator may recover from the party legally at fault for such injuries **just
> damages** together with ... funeral expenses..."

Three features, each fatal to unpriceability:
1. **"surviving to ... executor"** — this is a SURVIVAL action. The cause of action
   belongs to **the decedent**, continuing into the estate. It is NOT a survivors' claim.
2. **"just damages"** — unenumerated and open-ended. Contrast Ohio's five heads and the
   UK's two, all explicitly measured from the survivors' side.
3. **"whether INSTANTANEOUS or otherwise"** — decisive. German law CLOSES the door
   exactly here: Drs. 18/11397 states that on instantaneous death the victim *"loses at
   that moment the legal capacity required for a pain-and-suffering claim to arise"* —
   so no claim exists. **Connecticut holds it open.** No pain, no suffering, no
   experienced loss — and the estate still recovers.

**What is being compensated in an instantaneous death, if not the survivors' loss and
not the decedent's suffering? THE LOSS OF THE LIFE ITSELF.**

The Connecticut case-law gloss (the damages measure including "destruction of the
capacity to carry on and enjoy life's activities") is asserted from background knowledge
and **has NOT been verified in this session** — CourtListener dropped mid-attempt. Flagged

# ⭐ THE REPLACEMENT: TWO INCOMPATIBLE THEORIES OF WHAT A DEATH IS

The law does not have one answer. It has **two**, and jurisdictions **choose**:

| | **LOSS-TO-SURVIVORS** (Lord Campbell model) | **SURVIVAL** (Connecticut model) |
|---|---|---|
| whose claim? | the survivors' | **the decedent's**, via the estate |
| what is priced? | what the living lost | **what the dead lost** |
| the life itself | **not priced — forbidden (DE) / absent (OH, UK)** | **PRICED** |
| instantaneous death | Germany: no claim arises | CT: **claim arises anyway** |
| examples | UK FAA 1976; Ohio ORC 2125.02; German §844 BGB | **Conn. Gen. Stat. § 52-555** |

## The deeper finding — and it is the important one
> **THE EPISTEMIC SKELETON IS INVARIANT. THE VALUE SKELETON IS NOT.**

- The fact/law seam held in **every** system tested (5 foreign, 2 US states, blind,
  263 cases). It does not vary. It cannot be chosen.
- **Whether a life can be priced VARIES BY JURISDICTION.** Germany forbids it in statute.
  Connecticut does it as a matter of course.

This is exactly the shape one should expect: **epistemics is about what is possible;
values are about what is chosen.** Constraints of knowledge are universal. Constraints
of worth are legislated. The instrument now reads both, and reads them differently.

## What this does to Sina's axioms — both of them are IMPLEMENTED, in different countries
- **A3 (life is supreme / unpriceable)** → implemented as the German-UK-Ohio **taboo**:
  you may not put a number on a life.
- **A1 (beings move toward enjoyment)** → implemented in the **Connecticut** measure,
  which (subject to the verification gap above) compensates the destruction of the
  decedent's own **capacity to carry on and enjoy life's activities**. Sina's enjoyment
  axiom, written into a damages statute.

**The two axioms are inconsistent, and the world's legal systems have SPLIT on which one
to enact.** That is not a philosophical stalemate — it is a measurable, binary,
jurisdiction-level variable. And it means Sina's axioms are not wrong; they are
**contingent** — chosen, not forced.

## The new ruler for this domain (replaces R = D/V)
> **The PRICING-OF-LIFE INDEX: does the jurisdiction compensate the decedent's own lost
> life? (survival model = YES; loss-to-survivors model = NO.)**
> Binary, readable from statute, checkable in every jurisdiction on Earth.

**Standing prediction to test:** the split should track legal FAMILY and statutory
LINEAGE (Lord Campbell's Act 1846 descendants say NO; survival-statute jurisdictions say
YES), NOT wealth, religion, or legal tradition (common vs civil).
**Sharp version: England says NO and Connecticut says YES — and both are common law.**
So the split is NOT the common-law/civil-law divide that structures the epistemic
skeleton. **The two skeletons are carved along DIFFERENT joints.** Testable immediately:
survey survival-vs-loss-to-survivors across 20 jurisdictions.

## Standing falsifier list (updated)
- If a *survival*-model jurisdiction turns out NOT to price the life (i.e. "just damages"
  is construed to mean only pre-death suffering + economic loss), the split collapses and
  unpriceability is resurrected. **This is the live threat to the new claim — VERIFY the
  Connecticut case law.**

# ⭐⭐ ATTEMPT 36 — VERIFICATION GAP CLOSED. THE PERFECT INVERSION.

The flagged gap ("CT case-law gloss NOT verified — background knowledge only") is now
CLOSED from primary sources. **The assertion was correct, verbatim.**

## Source 1 — Ecker v. West Hartford, 205 Conn. 219 (1987) [read in full]
Establishes the BASELINE that reframes the whole domain:
> Connecticut has adhered for 131 years to **Baker v. Bolton** (1808, Lord Ellenborough):
> **"there is no civil right of action at common law for damages resulting from the
> death of a human being."**

**At common law, killing a person was NOT A CIVIL INJURY AT ALL.** You could sue over a
broken arm; you could not sue over a death. Not a price of zero — **no cause of action
existed.** Ecker: § 52-555 "creates liability where none formerly existed."

**=> UNPRICEABILITY IS THE DEFAULT. Every wrongful-death statute on Earth is a
LEGISLATIVE OVERRIDE of a common-law prohibition.** The statutes are the exceptions.

## Source 2 — Floyd v. Fruit Industries, 144 Conn. 659 (1957) [read in full]
An **instantaneous death** case — the exact scenario where Germany says no claim can
arise. Connecticut's Supreme Court holds:

1. > "Our wrongful death statute adds to that cause of action, as an element of damage,
   > **THE DEATH ITSELF**, which was not recognized as an element of damage at common law."
2. > "Damages for wrongful death, as such, are allowed as compensation for the
   > **DESTRUCTION OF THE DECEDENT'S CAPACITY TO CARRY ON LIFE'S ACTIVITIES**, including
   > his capacity to earn money."
3. > "**Some damages are recoverable for DEATH ITSELF, EVEN THOUGH INSTANTANEOUS,
   > WITHOUT REGARD TO EARNINGS OR EARNING CAPACITY.**"
4. > "damages ... are assessed on the basis of the **LOSS TO THE DECEDENT** had he lived,
   > and, except in that sense, **not on the basis of loss to his estate**."
5. Approving the trial court's charge: damages are **"not to be measured by the loss,
   sentimental or financial, to the decedent's wife and children."**

**Connecticut prices THE LIFE. With a floor that ignores earnings entirely.**

## ⭐ THE PERFECT INVERSION (fully verified, both directions, primary sources)
| | **GERMANY (Drs. 18/11397)** | **CONNECTICUT (Floyd)** |
|---|---|---|
| what IS priced | the survivors' grief + economic loss | **the decedent's own destroyed life** |
| what is **FORBIDDEN** | **"Bewertungen des verlorenen Lebens können nicht in die Bemessung einfließen"** — valuations of the lost life may not enter | **"not to be measured by the loss, sentimental or financial, to the decedent's wife and children"** |
| instantaneous death | **no claim arises** (victim lost legal capacity) | **claim arises anyway** |

> **Germany forbids pricing the life. Connecticut forbids pricing the survivors' loss.**
> **Two systems. Exactly opposite prohibitions. Both fully functioning.**

## Floyd names the HERMIT PROBLEM itself — and argues Connecticut is better for it
> "our law often works out much more fairly than do statutes patterned on Lord Campbell's
> Act, **where generally no damages at all are recoverable for the death of a breadwinner
> who leaves no surviving dependents**."

The court identified, in 1957, the exact failure mode this project derived from Ohio's
statute independently in attempt 33. **Connecticut chose its rule BECAUSE of it.**

## THE CORRECTED STRUCTURE — three distinct objects, not two
Earlier I conflated these. They are different:
1. **D_life** — the price of the LIFE, owed to the decedent. (Connecticut. FORBIDDEN in Germany.)
2. **D_grief** — the price of the SURVIVORS' suffering. (UK £15,120; German Hinterbliebenengeld. FORBIDDEN in Connecticut.)
3. **V** — economic transfers. (Present in all systems, computed individually.)

- **Lord Campbell family (UK, Ohio, Germany): D_grief + V. D_life FORBIDDEN.**
- **Survival family (Connecticut): D_life + V. D_grief FORBIDDEN.**
