# Armitage v. DeGroot (Idaho Sup. Ct., Oct. 28, 2026)
Source: CourtListener opinion 11515307, full text read (blind protocol, case id ca-armitage-degroot). Brody, C.J.; unanimous.
Campaign A: grade blind; prior exposure case name only. Committed CLEAN_AFFIRM / pure_law. Actual CLEAN_AFFIRM / pure_law. Notch hit.

Posture: sellers of a cabin sued buyers for the $100,000 personalty price after closing; bench trial for sellers (offset for missing items, unchallenged). Buyers raised merger-by-deed on reconsideration; district court rejected it and awarded fees under I.C. 12-120(3) ("bill").

Holdings:
1. Merger: district court wrong that the personalty agreement was not antecedent to the deed, but right result. Court states a two-part collateral-promise test (promise collateral = not about title, possession, parcel size or crops; parties intended survival). Payment for personalty is collateral and was meant to survive. Deed absent from record presumed to support the trial court. (de novo, pure law, held)
2. Fees: "and in any commercial transaction" in 12-120(3) is an independent basis, not a limit on the other categories (Eriksen adopted; Brower read narrowly). Fees affirmed; appellate fees to sellers. (pure law, held)

Findings of fact unchallenged on appeal.
