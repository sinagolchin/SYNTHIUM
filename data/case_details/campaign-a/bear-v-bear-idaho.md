# Bear v. Bear (Idaho Sup. Ct., Oct. 28, 2026, Docket 52888)
Source: CourtListener opinion 11515144, full text read (blind protocol, case id ca-bear-bear). Brody, C.J.; unanimous.
Campaign A: grade blind; prior exposure case name only. Committed REVERSED / pure_law. Actual CLEAN_AFFIRM / pure_law. Notch miss.

Posture: ex-wife sought partition of Idaho house held as tenants in common under a Montana divorce decree whose incorporated settlement agreement gives the Montana court exclusive jurisdiction over enforcement, modification or interpretation. District court dismissed for lack of subject-matter jurisdiction.

Holding: the SMJ ruling was wrong (Idaho district courts have SMJ over partition; Olsen v. Olsen's "jurisdiction" language is really preclusion). But the error was harmless: full faith and credit requires honouring the Montana decree's reservation, and partition here would require interpreting/enforcing the agreement ("sold upon the mutual agreement of the parties"). Dismissal without prejudice affirmed on alternative grounds; no appellate fees.

Hypothesis note: the law layer was corrected (the trial court's legal theory rejected) yet the disposition did not move. Under the notch coding this is CLEAN_AFFIRM; under a "did the law move" reading it is a law-side correction without outcome change. Worth flagging for the coding vocabulary proposal (P1).
