# AB v Newport City Council [2022] UKUT 190 (AAC) — UK Upper Tribunal, Admin Appeals Chamber
**Judge:** Upper Tribunal Judge Mitchell | **Court below:** Special Educational Needs
Tribunal for Wales | **Decided:** 2022-08-01 (heard 2021-10-25)

## Facts (paraphrased from full text)
Newport City Council stopped maintaining a statement of special educational needs
for a 17-year-old (S) with autism spectrum disorder, anxiety, and social-communication
difficulties, on the basis she had finished compulsory schooling. S was partway
through sixth-form A-Levels at an independent school (fees previously funded by the
council). Her parents appealed; the Welsh SEN Tribunal dismissed the appeal and later
refused to review its own decision. The parent appealed to the Upper Tribunal on four
grounds.

## Held (four grounds)
1. **Jurisdictional question, decided FOR the appellant:** contrary to the council's
   argument, there IS a right of appeal to the Upper Tribunal against a Tribunal
   review decision, not just against the original appeal decision — a genuine
   statutory-construction ruling on how two different Acts interlock.
2. Whether the Tribunal wrongly considered post-decision evidence on review: appellant
   technically right, but the error wasn't material — ground fails.
3 & 4. **The core failure:** the Tribunal never addressed whether S could cope with
   being transferred mid-A-Level to a split-site local school, despite this being a
   squarely relevant question under existing case law (W v. Gloucestershire). Inadequate
   reasons = legal error.

## Disposition
**Appeal ALLOWED. Tribunal's decision set aside** (no further order, since S had by
then finished her studies and the point had become moot between the parties — but
heard anyway because it raised a live legal question).

## Notable
The judge closes with pointed criticism of the council's conduct — inconsistent
justifications for ending the statement, describing the family's request as about
"prestige," and giving notice mid-summer with only two weeks before term.
