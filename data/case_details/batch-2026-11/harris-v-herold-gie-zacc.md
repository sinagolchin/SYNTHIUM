## Facts
Retired purchasers of life rights in a retirement scheme (St Leger Retirement Hotel) paid the purchase price into the
developer's attorneys' (HGB) trust account under s 6(3)(a) of the Housing Development Schemes for Retired Persons Act
65 of 1988. HGB released the funds to the developer before the s 6(1)(a) compliance certificate existed. Purchasers
cancelled in 2014; developer sequestrated 2016; purchasers got only a concurrent dividend. They sued HGB under s 6(4).

## Issue and reasoning
Single question of statutory construction (separated question 1): does s 6(4) ("any amount kept in a trust account
... shall immediately become payable to the purchaser") found a claim against the practitioner where the entrusted
funds were paid away before the developer's insolvency?
- Minority (Goosen AJ): no. Plain words mean funds still kept at insolvency; s 6(4) just ends the entrustment
  exception; purchasers' remedy is delict for breach of s 6(3)(a). Would have dismissed the appeal.
- Majority (Majiedt J): yes. Constitutional jurisdiction also engaged; social legislation read under s 39(2).
  s 6(3) creates a statutory escrow; "kept" is de jure, not de facto; an unlawful pay-away does not strip the
  purchaser of the s 6(4) remedy. Questions 2 and 3 not before the Court.

## Disposition: LEAVE GRANTED; APPEAL UPHELD "to the extent set out". SCA order set aside and replaced: HGB's appeal
to the SCA dismissed with costs; question 1 decided for the plaintiffs; question 3 referred back to the High Court;
matter remitted for further hearing. HGB pays costs in the CC.

## Seam reading
Pure law-side movement (meaning of "kept in a trust account"). No fact-finding at any appellate tier; the separated
question was framed as a point of law and the evidence-dependent question 3 was sent back down. The split (5-3) is
on interpretive method (text vs purposive/constitutional), not on facts.
