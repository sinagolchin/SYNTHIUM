## Facts
Former school caretaker, now 83, charged in 2020 with historic sexual offending against three pupils (1982-89).
A 1989 complaint was not pursued; a police video interview and his wife's statement are missing; many witnesses dead.
Dementia (Alzheimer's type) diagnosed; in April 2023 found unfit to stand trial under the CPMIP Act 2003.
Before the mandatory "involvement hearing" (s 8A(5), s 10(2): the court "must" inquire into involvement), defence
renewed a s 147 dismissal application based on delay, lost evidence, age and impairment. DC dismissed the charges.
CA (on Crown appeal) held the CPMIP Act is a code: after unfitness, dismissal/stay must rest on matters independent
of the disability (following R v M [2002] 1 WLR 824).

## Issue and reasoning
Pure question of statutory construction / jurisdiction: does the word "must" in ss 8A(5) and 10(2) oust the ordinary
s 147 dismissal and inherent stay powers once a defendant is found unfit?
Majority: construe the two schemes to work together; Bill of Rights s 6 (rights-consistent reading), s 25 fair trial,
s 19 non-discrimination. An unfit defendant keeps the ordinary right to seek stay/dismissal on grounds not solely
dependent on unfitness (delay, physical impairment, misconduct). Where the application relies wholly on the same
mental impairment, the CPMIP pathway must be followed. Where other grounds are substantially engaged, stay/dismissal
remains open (R v B, Codd, Corkran approved). "Must" only fixes the order: involvement before disposition.
Applied: prerequisites met (delay; physical conditions). But the DC relied mainly on the mental-impairment reports,
did not identify substantial other grounds, and did not consider stay rather than dismissal (dismissal = deemed acquittal).
Miller J: same result; differs on method (grounds must be independent; judge asks overall whether a stay would be
granted were it not for the qualifying impairment).

## Disposition: APPEAL ALLOWED. Neither the DC nor the CA judgment stands. The s 147 application is REMITTED to the
District Court for rehearing.

## Seam reading
Movement is entirely law-side (construction of "must"; jurisdiction). The apex court expressly declined to decide the
fact-laden question itself: "we do not [have sufficient information] ... particularly as to the effects of delay on fair
trial, and the extent of the effects of Mr Bailey's physical impairments" → remitted. Fact evaluation returned to the
first-instance court rather than being done at the apex. Same structural pattern as the Japan finding (C1): apex fixes
law, facts are found below.
