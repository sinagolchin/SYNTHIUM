# Estate of Stephen M. Jennions v. CFTC, No. 25-1106 (U.S. Court of Appeals, D.C. Circuit, 2026-07-28) — FULL TEXT VERIFIED
**Bench:** Pillard, Wilkins, Katsas, Circuit Judges. Opinion by Wilkins J; no separate opinions. Argued April 6, 2026.
**Below:** CFTC Claims Review Staff preliminary determination (Feb 9, 2022) → Commission Final Determinations (Mar 12, 2025) denying a whistleblower award for the November 2014 FX-benchmark covered actions ($1.4bn penalties).
**Source:** CourtListener opinion 11403296, cluster 10935724. Full text (30,257 chars) read in entirety.

## Facts
Jennions gave information on WM/R 4 p.m. fix manipulation to the UK FCA in January 2013. A Bloomberg article
(June 12, 2013) described the scheme in detail and quoted one FCA line ("aware of these allegations"). The CFTC opened
its investigation about June 19, 2013, citing the article. Jennions never contacted the CFTC until a TCR filed in
February 2015, after the covered actions. He sought an award under 17 C.F.R. § 165.2(i)(1) and alleged undue
influence by the CFTC's Office of General Counsel on the claims review.

## Issue and reasoning
Review under 7 U.S.C. § 26(f)(3) and APA § 706(2)(A); for agency fact-finding, arbitrary-and-capricious equals
substantial evidence (Crooks v. Mabus).
- Causation: substantial evidence supports the Commission's finding that the article's detailed account, not the
  one-line FCA quote, prompted the investigation. Even assuming the quote carried Jennions's information, it was not
  "specific" (Kitchen v. CFTC, 2026). The foreign-authority pathway (§ 165.2(l)(2)) was not raised and not reached.
- Undue influence: no record evidence, only speculation; and in any event no prejudice shown (Schaefer v. McHugh;
  Jicarilla; 5 U.S.C. § 706 harmless-error rule).

## Disposition: PETITION FOR REVIEW DENIED. Commission's denial stands in full.

## Seam reading
This is the fact side of the seam. The court treats the core question (what caused the investigation) as agency
fact-finding and applies the deferential substantial-evidence lens; the petitioner loses because he cannot show
the finding is implausible. The one law-flavoured point (the meaning of "specific" in the rule) is decided in the
agency's favour as an alternative ground. The procedural claim fails on prejudice, a remedy-side gate. Contrast
with Goncalves (US-MA), where the law-side count moved and the fact-side count held.
