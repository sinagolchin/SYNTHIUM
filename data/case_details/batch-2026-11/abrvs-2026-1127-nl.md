## Facts
Asylum applicant appealed. Added ground: the combined asylum decision with an embedded return decision was not
signed. He relied on ABRvS 6 Nov 2023 (ECLI:NL:RVS:2023:4082), where an unsigned stand-alone return decision
(model form M107-A) was defective because the person could not check that a competent official made it.

## Issue and reasoning
Question of law with an evidential application: is an unsigned combined asylum decision defective?
- Signature is not a statutory requirement; what matters is that the decision is knowable and reviewable
  ("kenbaar en toetsbaar"), i.e. that one can check a competent official made it. The type of mandate is irrelevant
  (correcting a possible reading by the rechtbank).
- Stand-alone return decisions are issued on a form designed for a signature; combined asylum decisions are drafted
  and approved in the IND's digital system and sent automatically.
- Here the official's name appears on the decision; a screenshot from the IND system "Indigo" shows that official
  finalised it and works in a team mandated under the Mandaatbesluit IND 2022. So competence is verifiable: no defect.
- All other grounds rejected without reasons under art. 91(2) Vw 2000 (no question of legal unity or development).

## Disposition: HOGER BEROEP ONGEGROND; the rechtbank's judgment is CONFIRMED ("bevestigt de aangevallen uitspraak").
No costs order.

## Seam reading
The appellate court restates the legal test (verifiability, not signature) and applies it itself to documentary
evidence the minister supplied on appeal (the Indigo screenshot). This is law-side review with the apex court doing
the fact application, not deferring to a fact-finder below; contrast with Jennions (US), where causation was
treated as agency fact-finding under deferential review.
Limit: the art. 91(2) dismissal of the remaining grounds is unreasoned, so their layer cannot be coded.
