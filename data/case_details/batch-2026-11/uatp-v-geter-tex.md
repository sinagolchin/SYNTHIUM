# UATP Management, LLC v. Geter, No. 24-0905 (Supreme Court of Texas, 2026-10-16, per curiam) — FULL TEXT VERIFIED

## Facts
A mother signed a release, indemnity and arbitration agreement for her minor daughter at an Urban Air trampoline
park; the daughter was injured; the mother sued the park and the affiliated "protected parties" named in the release
and indemnity clause but who did not sign. The arbitration clause refers only to undefined "parties".

## Issue and reasoning
All questions of law, reviewed de novo:
- The agreement binds the child because the parent signed it for her (Tex. Fam. Code § 151.001(a)(7); Taylor
  Morrison v. Ha). Direct-benefits estoppel was unnecessary. The child cannot disavow it after taking the benefit
  (Dairyland v. Roman).
- Whether a non-signatory may invoke the clause is a gateway question for the court, not the arbitrator, despite a
  delegation clause (Cerna; G.T. Leach).
- The protected parties are intended third-party beneficiaries: the release names them, the arbitration clause does
  not exclude them, and arbitration is the chosen mechanism for enforcing the release (whole-contract reading, Valence).

## Disposition: PETITION GRANTED; the portion of the court of appeals' judgment affirming denial of arbitration as to the
protected parties is REVERSED; the unchallenged remainder stands; remanded to the trial court.

## Seam reading
