## Facts
Purchaser (appellant) bought property from the respondent developer and later sued for misrepresentation
(damages under s 2(1) Misrepresentation Act; damages in lieu of rescission under s 2(2); equitable rescission for
innocent misrepresentation). Respondent applied to strike out the claim as time-barred under the Limitation Act.
Key factual question: when the appellant had the requisite knowledge (respondent argued 16 May 2018).

## Issue and reasoning
Pure questions of law on a strike-out:
- s 2(2) MA is not a free-standing cause of action and needs a subsisting right to rescind (appellant LOST this point).
- A s 2(1) claim is "founded on tort" under s 6(1)(a) LA (and falls within s 6(1)(d) too): 6-year period.
- Equitable rescission for innocent misrepresentation lies in equity's exclusive jurisdiction, so s 6(7) does not
  apply: no statutory limitation period, only laches (not raised).
- Misrepresentation is a species of mistake, so postponement under s 29(1)(c) is available; s 24A does not displace
  s 6 read with s 29(1)(c).
- Applied to the strike-out standard: not "plain and obvious" the claim is time-barred; knowledge as at 16 May 2018
  is a question of fact for trial.

## Disposition: APPEAL ALLOWED. Strike-out set aside; the AR's order reinstated (incl. AR costs $9,700).
Costs of the appeal $45,000 to the appellant. Claim proceeds to trial.

## Seam reading
All movement is law-side (construction of the Misrepresentation Act and Limitation Act; exclusive vs concurrent
equitable jurisdiction). The factual question (date of knowledge) was expressly NOT decided and is pushed down to
trial. Same structural pattern as Bailey (NZ): the apex court fixes the law and leaves the facts to the court below.
