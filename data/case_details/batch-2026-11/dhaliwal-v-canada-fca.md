# Dhaliwal v. Canada (Attorney General), 2026 FCA 158, A-36-25 (Federal Court of Appeal, 2026-09-23) — FULL TEXT VERIFIED
**Bench:** Locke, Goyette, Pamel JJ.A. Reasons of the Court by Locke J.A., delivered from the bench at Montréal on the hearing day. Appellant self-represented.
**Below:** CRA found the appellant ineligible for the Canada Recovery Benefit and the Canada Emergency Response Benefit → Federal Court refused an extension of time to bring judicial review.
**Source:** Legal Data Hunter, CA/A2AJ id "2026 FCA 158"; https://decisions.fca-caf.gc.ca/fca-caf/decisions/en/item/521907/index.do. Full text (paras [1]-[10] and the counsel page) read in entirety.

## Facts
The appellant sought more time to challenge the CRA's benefit-eligibility decision. The Federal Court applied the
four Larkman questions (2012 FCA 204, para 61) and found three for her (continuing intention, no prejudice,
reasonable explanation for delay) but held she showed no potential merit: she identified no grounds. It disregarded
her reply, which contained unsworn factual allegations, as improper.

## Issue and reasoning
- Standard: the extension decision is discretionary and a question of mixed fact and law; absent an extricable
  question of law, the FCA intervenes only for palpable and overriding error.
- The Federal Court did not demand a written draft application, only some indication of grounds; none was given.
- The reply was properly disregarded: unsupported by affidavit, raised only in reply (no chance for the Crown to
  answer), and no procedural-fairness reason to admit it.
- Fresh-evidence motion dismissed: it went to delay, not to merit, which was the decisive issue.
- It was open to the Federal Court to treat the lack of any ground as determinative.

## Disposition: APPEAL DISMISSED, with all-inclusive costs of $500. Fresh-evidence motion dismissed.

## Seam reading
Clear fact-side deference: the court names the Housen standard (palpable and overriding) and finds no extricable
legal error. The one legal point (whether a written draft was required) is resolved by reading what the judge below
actually did, not by announcing a new rule. The CRA's underlying administrative merits were never reached.
