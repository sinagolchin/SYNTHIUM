MOELLER, Justice.




                                                 1
       This appeal arises from a dispute over snow removal and other maintenance along a
segment of a common road in rural Teton County, Idaho. Craig Colton sued the Overlook at Fox
Creek Subdivision Owners’ Association (“the Overlook HOA”), GNJ Properties, LLC (“GNJ”)
(the developer of that subdivision), Jeffrey Flowers, Rhonda G. Flowers, David Hewes, Lee
Naylon, William Kyle Reichart, Sarah Flynn, Caroline Knobelspies, Linda Suzanne Swan, and
Charles Woodward (the “individual Respondents”) (all collectively “Respondents”), seeking a
declaratory judgment that either: (1) the Overlook HOA is responsible for all road maintenance
costs, or (2) those costs must be shared equitably among all parcel owners who use the road.
Respondents filed an answer and a counterclaim seeking a declaratory judgment of their own. The
district court later granted Respondents’ motion to dismiss the complaint and granted their motion
for summary judgment on their counterclaims, ruling that because the disputed portions of the road
are not within the boundaries of the subdivision, the Overlook HOA is not responsible for the costs.
       On appeal, Colton argues that the district court erred in several respects. First, he contends
it failed to address his alternative argument: that all neighboring property owners who use the road
should share responsibility for its maintenance costs. Instead, it dismissed his claims solely based
on his lack of standing as to one party—the Overlook HOA. Second, Colton maintains that the
court erred in concluding that the Overlook HOA has no obligation to contribute to the road’s
maintenance costs based on its finding that the road is not a subdivision road. Third, he avers that
the court erred in awarding attorney fees and costs to Respondents on two occasions. For the
reasons explained below, we reverse and remand this matter to the district court for further
proceedings. The judgments awarding attorney fees are also vacated.
                    I.    FACTUAL AND PROCEDURAL BACKGROUND
       Colton owns a home and property in Teton County, Idaho, located in the Spruce Hill
Subdivision, a single lot subdivision (“Spruce Hill”). See property identified in magenta in Figure
1, infra. His home sits at the end of a road identified as East 5250 South. See road identified in
yellow in Figure 1, infra. Part of the road is a public county road (the east-west trajectory) and part
of the road is created by partial public easements (the north-south trajectory). Teton County’s
portion of the road begins at the intersection of East 5250 South and South 750 in Teton County
and ends at the southwest corner of Lot 14 in the Overlook at Fox Creek Subdivision (the
“Overlook Subdivision” or “Subdivision”). There are two right-of-way easements on East 5250
South that also provide access to Spruce Hill and to a property neighboring Spruce Hill. According

Ranch entry gate,” the location of which is not clear from the record. The remaining unplowed
portion of the road is at issue in this case (“the Roadway”).

declaration that the complaint be dismissed. GNJ and the individual Respondents later joined in
the HOA’s answer and counterclaim. On October 23, 2023, Respondents filed a motion to dismiss
Colton’s complaint under Idaho Rules of Civil Procedure 7, 8, and 12(b)(6). After a hearing, the
district court entered an order granting Respondents’ motion to dismiss after concluding that
Colton did not have standing to bring a declaratory judgment action pertaining to the CC&amp;Rs or



                                                 4
the Development Agreement because he was not in privity of contract with the Overlook HOA
through the CC&amp;Rs or with GNJ through the Development Agreement. On March 15, 2024, the
district court also issued an order and amended judgment awarding attorney fees and costs to
Respondents.
       Subsequently, on June 12, 2024, Respondents filed a motion for summary judgment on
their counterclaim. Colton filed an opposition to Respondents’ motion on June 24, 2024. The
district court granted Respondents’ motion for summary judgment in part and denied it in part.
After declining to formally declare the road as a county or subdivision road, it determined that
because there was insufficient evidence in the record to establish that East 5250 South was located

not have a contractual duty to provide snow removal or maintenance to Colton, (2) that the
Development Agreement did not create an ongoing obligation for the HOA to maintain, repair, or
plow the road, and (3) that the HOA has a right, not an obligation, to implement a noxious weed
program. Final judgment in favor of Respondents on the counterclaims was entered on November
27, 2024. The district court later awarded additional attorney fees and costs to Respondents. Colton
timely appealed.
                              II.    STANDARDS OF REVIEW
       A district court’s dismissal of a complaint pursuant to Idaho Rule of Civil Procedure
12(b)(6) and grant of summary judgment under Rule 56 are both reviewed de novo. Paslay v. A&amp;B
Irrigation Dist., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9879402"><a href="/opinion/4447197/daniel-paslay-v-ab-irrigation-district/#868" aria-description="Citation for case: Daniel Paslay v. A&amp;B Irrigation District">162 Idaho 866, 868</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9879402"><a href="/opinion/4447197/daniel-paslay-v-ab-irrigation-district/#880" aria-description="Citation for case: Daniel Paslay v. A&amp;B Irrigation District">406 P.3d 878, 880</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2017).
       Rule 12(b)(6) provides that, when considering a motion to dismiss, the court must first
“view[] all facts and inferences from the record in favor of the non-moving party, [then] the Court
will ask whether a claim for relief has been stated.” Losser v. Bradstreet, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2581193"><a href="/opinion/2581193/losser-v-bradstreet/#673" aria-description="Citation for case: Losser v. Bradstreet">145 Idaho 670, 673</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2581193"><a href="/opinion/2581193/losser-v-bradstreet/#761" aria-description="Citation for case: Losser v. Bradstreet">183
P.3d 758, 761</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2008) (quoting Gallagher v. State, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2575967"><a href="/opinion/2575967/gallagher-v-state/#667" aria-description="Citation for case: Gallagher v. State">141 Idaho 665, 667</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2575967"><a href="/opinion/2575967/gallagher-v-state/#758" aria-description="Citation for case: Gallagher v. State">115 P.3d 756, 758</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2005)).
“A 12(b)(6) motion looks only at the pleadings to determine whether a claim for relief has been
stated and this Court determines whether the non-movant has alleged sufficient facts in support of
his claim, which if true, would entitle him to relief.” Boren v. Gadwa, </pre><span class="citation no-link">176 Idaho 489</span><pre class="inline">, ___, </pre><span class="citation no-link">578
P.3d 861</span><pre class="inline">, 868–69 (2024) (internal quotation marks omitted) (quoting Fulfer v. Sorrento Lactalis,
Inc., </pre><span class="citation no-link">171 Idaho 296</span><pre class="inline">, 300, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="11199356"><a href="/opinion/10732771/fulfer-v-sorrento-lactalis-inc/#712" aria-description="Citation for case: Fulfer v. Sorrento Lactalis, Inc.">520 P.3d 708, 712</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2022)). “[A] complaint should not be dismissed for
failure to state a claim unless it appears beyond doubt that the plaintiff can prove no set of facts in



                                                  5
support of his claim which would entitle him to relief.” Gardner v. Hollifield, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="1236034"><a href="/opinion/1236034/gardner-v-hollifield/#611" aria-description="Citation for case: Gardner v. Hollifield">96 Idaho 609, 611</a></span><pre class="inline">,
</pre><span class="citation" data-id="1236034"><a href="/opinion/1236034/gardner-v-hollifield/#732" aria-description="Citation for case: Gardner v. Hollifield">533 P.2d 730, 732</a></span><pre class="inline"> (1975).
       Summary judgment is only appropriate where “the pleadings, depositions, and admissions
on file, together with the affidavits, if any, show that there is no genuine issue as to any material
fact and that the moving party is entitled to a judgment as a matter of law.” Friel v. Boise City
Hous. Auth., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="1160125"><a href="/opinion/1160125/friel-v-boise-city-housing-authority/#485" aria-description="Citation for case: Friel v. Boise City Housing Authority">126 Idaho 484, 485</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="1160125"><a href="/opinion/1160125/friel-v-boise-city-housing-authority/#30" aria-description="Citation for case: Friel v. Boise City Housing Authority">887 P.2d 29, 30</a></span><pre class="inline"> (1994) (citation omitted); I.R.C.P. 56(a). “When
considering whether the evidence shows a genuine issue of material fact, the trial court must
liberally construe the facts, and draw all reasonable inferences in favor of the nonmoving party.”
Spears v. Antelope Mountain Resort, LLC, </pre><span class="citation no-link">177 Idaho 70</span><pre class="inline">, ___, </pre><span class="citation no-link">585 P.3d 314</span><pre class="inline">, 320 (2026) (citation
omitted). “To establish that a genuine dispute of material fact exists, a party must present evidence
upon which a jury could reasonably rely; a ‘scintilla of evidence merely casting a slight doubt of
the facts’ will not suffice.” </pre><span class="citation no-link">Id.</span><pre class="inline"> (quoting Fragnella v. Petrovich, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="880186"><a href="/opinion/880186/tiffany-ann-marie-fragnella-v-robert-b-petrovich-jr/#271" aria-description="Citation for case: Tiffany Ann Marie Fragnella v. Robert B. Petrovich, Jr.">153 Idaho 266, 271</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="880186"><a href="/opinion/880186/tiffany-ann-marie-fragnella-v-robert-b-petrovich-jr/#108" aria-description="Citation for case: Tiffany Ann Marie Fragnella v. Robert B. Petrovich, Jr.">281 P.3d 103,
108</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2012)).
       “The awarding of attorney fees and costs is within the discretion of the trial court and
subject to review for an abuse of discretion.” Smith v. Mitton, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2639987"><a href="/opinion/2639987/smith-v-mitton/#897" aria-description="Citation for case: Smith v. Mitton">140 Idaho 893, 897</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2639987"><a href="/opinion/2639987/smith-v-mitton/#371" aria-description="Citation for case: Smith v. Mitton">104 P.3d 367,
371</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2004). In determining whether the court abused its discretion, this Court asks “[w]hether the
trial court: (1) correctly perceived the issue as one of discretion; (2) acted within the outer
boundaries of its discretion; (3) acted consistently with the legal standards applicable to the
specific choices available to it; and (4) reached its decision by the exercise of reason.” Lunneborg
v. My Fun Life, </pre><span class="citation no-link">163 Idaho 856</span><pre class="inline">, 863, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="4289457"><a href="/opinion/4512204/lunneborg-v-my-fun-life-corp/#194" aria-description="Citation for case: Lunneborg v. My Fun Life, Corp.">421 P.3d 187, 194</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2018).
                                      III.    ANALYSIS
A. We reverse the district court’s dismissal of Colton’s claims.
       In addressing the scope of Colton’s claims, the district court first concluded that Colton’s
only claim was centered on the enforceability of the Overlook CC&amp;Rs and the Development
Agreement against Respondents. In its analysis of whether Colton had standing to bring this claim,
the court first determined that Colton’s complaint sufficiently alleged an injury in fact (economic
damage due to the cost of maintaining of the road). However, the court then concluded that Colton
could not demonstrate a sufficient causal connection between the complained-of conduct and the
alleged injury. It explained that because Colton is not a contracting party to either the Development
Agreement or the Overlook CC&amp;Rs, he has no privity of contract and, thus, no standing to request
relief on the basis of those documents.



                                                 6
        Colton now argues on appeal that the district court erred when it dismissed his complaint
based solely on its ruling that he lacked standing. He claims that his complaint sufficiently pleaded
a claim based in equity and based on the law of easements, rather than just on the terms of the
CC&amp;Rs and the Development Agreement, but the court failed to consider those issues. He also
asserts that, aside from the Overlook HOA, there were ten other defendants who accessed their
property over the Roadway, and the district court dismissed his case against all of them based
solely on his standing with one defendant.
        When ruling on contested matters, district courts “must address all the issues the parties
present.” Evans v. Wright, </pre><span class="citation no-link">174 Idaho 330</span><pre class="inline">, 340, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="11199303"><a href="/opinion/10732718/evans-v-wright/#601" aria-description="Citation for case: Evans v. Wright">554 P.3d 591, 601</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2024) (concluding that the
district court erred in failing to address one of the appellant’s arguments when granting the
respondents’ motions to dismiss). Here, the district court summarily concluded that Colton only
stated one claim of relief in his complaint: “Colton raises a single claim and asks this [c]ourt for a
declaratory judgment . . . .” However, it overlooked the alternative request in Colton’s complaint
as to whether the neighboring property owners in the HOA who use and benefit from the Roadway
must share in the costs of the Roadway’s maintenance. We conclude that the district court erred in
its failure to address this issue.
        Idaho’s Uniform Declaratory Judgment Act provides that “[c]ourts of record within their
respective jurisdictions shall have power to declare rights, status, and other legal relations, whether
or not further relief is or could be claimed.” I.C. § 10-1201. To state a claim for declaratory relief
under the statute, a plaintiff may allege that they are “any person interested”: (1) “under a deed,
will, written contract or other writings constituting a contract or any oral contract,” or (2) “whose
rights, status or other legal relations are affected by a statute, municipal ordinance, contract or
franchise[.]” I.C. § 10-1202. Contrary to Respondents’ argument on appeal, Colton’s failure to
allege his status as an interested party under Idaho Code section 10-1202 is not dispositive. Section
10-1202 merely identifies certain categories of persons who may seek declaratory relief, while
section 10-1205 expressly provides that the enumeration in section 10-1202 “does not limit or
restrict the exercise of the general powers conferred in [s]ection 10-1201, in any proceedings where
declaratory relief is sought, in which a judgment or decree will terminate the controversy or remove
an uncertainty.” I.C. § 10-1205. Thus, the question is not whether Colton adequately alleged that
he is a “person interested.” Rather, we must first determine whether he sufficiently alleged an




                                                  7
actual controversy regarding the parties’ respective rights and obligations to maintain the
Roadway, such that declaratory relief would resolve the dispute.
       We first turn to Colton’s complaint to determine whether he sufficiently alleged a
justiciable controversy supporting declaratory relief under Idaho’s pleading standards. Under
Idaho Rule of Civil Procedure 8(a), “a claim for relief must contain: . . . (2) a short and plain
statement of the claim showing that the pleader is entitled to relief . . . .” I.R.C.P. 8(a)(2). “Each
allegation must be simple, concise, and direct.” I.R.C.P. 8(d)(1). Idaho’s notice-pleading
requirement only “requires a pleading to put the adverse party ‘on notice of the claims brought
against it.’ ” Noell Indus., Inc. v. Idaho State Tax Comm’n, </pre><span class="citation no-link">167 Idaho 367</span><pre class="inline">, 371, </pre><span class="citation no-link">470 P.3d 1176</span><pre class="inline">,
1180 (2020) (quoting Hodge ex rel. Welch v. Waggoner, </pre><span class="citation no-link">164 Idaho 89</span><pre class="inline">, 96, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9886923"><a href="/opinion/4521454/hodge-v-waggoner/#1239" aria-description="Citation for case: Hodge v. Waggoner">425 P.3d 1232, 1239</a></span><pre class="inline">
(2018)). “Accordingly, notice pleading requires the complaint to provide ‘some indication’ of the
basis for relief, but not always an exact statutory basis or formal cause of action.” </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9886923"><a href="/opinion/4521454/hodge-v-waggoner/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hodge v. Waggoner">Id.</a></span><pre class="inline"> (quoting
Brown v. City of Pocatello, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="874275"><a href="/opinion/874275/brown-v-city-of-pocatello/#808" aria-description="Citation for case: Brown v. City of Pocatello">148 Idaho 802, 808</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="874275"><a href="/opinion/874275/brown-v-city-of-pocatello/#1170" aria-description="Citation for case: Brown v. City of Pocatello">229 P.3d 1164, 1170</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2010)). Our liberal pleading
standard “prevent[s] the dismissal of a valid claim for a mere technical failing.” Brown, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="874275"><a href="/opinion/874275/brown-v-city-of-pocatello/#809" aria-description="Citation for case: Brown v. City of Pocatello">148 Idaho
at 809</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="874275"><a href="/opinion/874275/brown-v-city-of-pocatello/#1171" aria-description="Citation for case: Brown v. City of Pocatello">229 P.3d at 1171</a></span><pre class="inline">.
       Moreover, “[a] prerequisite to a declaratory judgment action is an actual or justiciable
controversy.” Miles v. Idaho Power Co., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9855450"><a href="/opinion/1298150/miles-v-idaho-power-co-ex-rel-evans/#639" aria-description="Citation for case: Miles v. Idaho Power Co. Ex Rel. Evans">116 Idaho 635, 639</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9855450"><a href="/opinion/1298150/miles-v-idaho-power-co-ex-rel-evans/#761" aria-description="Citation for case: Miles v. Idaho Power Co. Ex Rel. Evans">778 P.2d 757, 761</a></span><pre class="inline"> (1989). “[T]o
invoke the trial court’s jurisdiction in a declaratory judgment action, the plaintiffs must allege a
‘personal stake’ in the outcome of the controversy, which ‘has come to be understood to require
not only a “distinct palpable injury” to the plaintiff, but also a “fairly traceable” causal connection
between the claimed injury and the challenged conduct.’ ” Idaho Branch Inc. of Associated Gen.
Contractors of Am., Inc. v. Nampa Highway Dist. No. 1, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9604166"><a href="/opinion/1367562/idaho-branch-inc-of-associated-general-contractors-of-america-inc-v/#242" aria-description="Citation for case: Idaho Branch, Inc. of Associated General Contractors of...">123 Idaho 237, 242</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9604166"><a href="/opinion/1367562/idaho-branch-inc-of-associated-general-contractors-of-america-inc-v/#244" aria-description="Citation for case: Idaho Branch, Inc. of Associated General Contractors of...">846 P.2d 239, 244</a></span><pre class="inline">
(Ct. App. 1993) (quoting Miles, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9855450"><a href="/opinion/1298150/miles-v-idaho-power-co-ex-rel-evans/#641" aria-description="Citation for case: Miles v. Idaho Power Co. Ex Rel. Evans">116 Idaho at 641</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9855450"><a href="/opinion/1298150/miles-v-idaho-power-co-ex-rel-evans/#763" aria-description="Citation for case: Miles v. Idaho Power Co. Ex Rel. Evans">778 P.2d at 763</a></span><pre class="inline">); see also Nemeth v. Shoshone
County, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="4459131"><a href="/opinion/10732905/nemeth-v-shoshone-county/" aria-description="Citation for case: Nemeth v. Shoshone County">165 Idaho 851</a></span><pre class="inline">, 860–61, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="4459131"><a href="/opinion/10732905/nemeth-v-shoshone-county/" aria-description="Citation for case: Nemeth v. Shoshone County">453 P.3d 844</a></span><pre class="inline">, 853–54 (2019) (holding plaintiffs had a personal
stake in obtaining a declaration concerning roadway access to their property). Importantly, the
requirement for a “personal stake in the outcome” also contemplates that the claimed injury is
redressable by the relief requested by the injured party. That element is met here because a judicial
declaration apportioning responsibility for the Roadway’s maintenance among the respective
parties would redress the injury Colton alleges from bearing the maintenance costs alone.
       Turning next to Colton’s claims against the HOA and GNJ, which the district court
dismissed for lack of standing, we hold that the district court erred. Idaho Code section 10-1202



                                                  8
states, in relevant part: “Any person . . . whose rights, status or other legal relations are affected
by a . . . contract . . . may have determined any question of construction or validity arising under
the . . . contract . . . and obtain a declaration of rights, status or other legal relationships thereunder.”
I.C. § 10-1202. The statute permits declaratory relief not only for a person “interested under” a
contract, but also for a person whose legal rights are “affected by” the contract. Id. In City of
Middleton v. Coleman Homes, LLC, we held that, despite the fact that “all four [defendants] were
not signatories to the [a]greements, all four alleged they were interested parties and involved with
the [a]greements”; thus, the district court did not err in treating them as such when issuing a
declaratory judgment. </pre><span class="citation no-link">163 Idaho 716</span><pre class="inline">, 727, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="4276622"><a href="/opinion/4499369/city-of-middleton-v-coleman-homes-llc/#1236" aria-description="Citation for case: City of Middleton v. Coleman Homes, LLC">418 P.3d 1225, 1236</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2018).
        Here, as alleged in Colton’s complaint, the CC&amp;Rs impose a duty on another party to
contribute to the plowing and maintenance costs of the Roadway. Even though Colton is not
himself a party to that agreement, whether that obligation exists and is enforceable has a direct
financial effect on Colton because the HOA and GNJ are not contributing to the costs—Colton has
been bearing all the road-plowing expenses himself. Thus, the legal question of whether the
disputed portion of the Roadway falls within the obligations the HOA and GNJ assumed under the
CC&amp;Rs and Development Agreement is key to ascertaining Colton’s own legal and financial
responsibilities for the Roadway. In sum, Colton’s interest in the construction of the CC&amp;Rs and
Development Agreement is sufficient to seek declaratory relief to determine the parties’ respective
rights and obligations under Idaho Code section 10-1202.
        Finally, we consider whether Colton alleged sufficient facts to establish standing as to his
claims against the individual Respondents. Colton alleged in his complaint that he and the
individual Respondents share common interests in the Roadway and make use of it pursuant to an
easement. For example, Colton avers in his complaint:
        E 5250 S is a public road that is not maintained or plowed by the County and it
        provides the sole access to seven lots in the Overlook at Fox Creek Subdivision and
        three private parcels.
               . . . While E 5250 S is a public roadway, under Idaho Code [s]ection 40-
        117(9): “the government may keep a public right-of-way open to the public without
        assuming an obligation to maintain it.”
                ....
                 . . . [Colton] subsequently sent letters to all the persons who own property
        which is solely accessed by this segment of road E 5250 S, to notify them of the
        plowing costs, of the HOA’s refusal to contribute and their potential individual
        liabilities for the costs.

              [Colton], as the owner of property that shares ingress and egress access with
       several property owners on the road known as E 5250 S, a county road, has a
       personal stake in the outcome of this controversy . . . .
               ....
               . . . [A]lthough[] the Defendants use this segment of E 5250 S as the sole
       access to their property, they have refused to participate in the required snow-
       plowing and upkeep of the road in any substantial way. Instead, they leave the
       burden upon just three property owners (two of which are located at the end of this
       road) and essentially, take the stance that [Colton] has to keep the entire segment
       of E 5250 S open in order to get to his home, so he can bear the burden and we will
       benefit from his efforts without any expense to ourselves.
In sum, Colton alleged that multiple property owners use the Roadway as their sole means of
access, yet he has solely borne the cost of maintaining it. His final alternative request is: “that the
court rule that all of the subdivision and non-subdivision owners served by this segment of county
road (not publicly maintained) should share said costs as an equitable and fair remedy.” Construing
the complaint liberally, this allegation was sufficient to place the individual Respondents on notice
that Colton sought a judicial determination regarding the respective obligations of each individual
party for maintenance of the Roadway.
       In reviewing a motion to dismiss under Idaho Rule of Civil Procedure 12(b)(6), this Court
is not tasked with determining whether Colton will ultimately prevail on his claim. Rather, the
question is whether the complaint alleges facts sufficient to state a plausible claim of relief.
Hammer v. Ribi, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="4199132"><a href="/opinion/4421879/sharon-r-hammer-v-nils-ribi/#573" aria-description="Citation for case: Sharon R. Hammer v. Nils Ribi">162 Idaho 570, 573</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="4199132"><a href="/opinion/4421879/sharon-r-hammer-v-nils-ribi/#151" aria-description="Citation for case: Sharon R. Hammer v. Nils Ribi">401 P.3d 148, 151</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2017). Accepting the allegations as true
and drawing all reasonable inferences in Colton’s favor, the complaint alleges that, although the
parties shared use of the Roadway, Colton individually incurred and paid all the expenses in
maintaining and plowing it, and that a dispute existed regarding whether Respondents were
obligated to contribute to the cost. Whether the easement at issue here ultimately imposes the
obligations asserted by Colton is a question for resolution on the merits, not on a motion to dismiss.
       Colton also sufficiently alleged a personal stake in the controversy. The complaint alleged
that he incurred expenses maintaining the Roadway and that Respondents benefited from and used
the Roadway while failing to bear their alleged share of the costs. Accepting those allegations as
true, Colton alleged a distinct and palpable injury in the maintenance costs, a conclusion also
reached by the district court. The complaint also alleged a causal connection between Colton’s
asserted injury and Respondents’ failure to contribute to the Roadway’s maintenance, despite




                                                  10
benefiting from its use. At the pleading stage, those allegations are sufficient to establish Colton’s
standing.
       Although the complaint lacks precision in certain respects, its allegations were sufficient
to survive a motion to dismiss. Thus, we reverse the district court’s order dismissing Colton’s
complaint for a declaratory judgment.
B. We reverse the district court’s grant of summary judgment in favor of the Overlook HOA
   on its counterclaim for declaratory judgment.
       Next, we turn to the district court’s ruling on summary judgment that the Overlook CC&amp;Rs
do not create an obligation for the Overlook HOA to maintain the Roadway. After once again
finding that Colton did not have standing to seek enforcement of the CC&amp;Rs, the court addressed
the Overlook HOA’s counterclaim asking it to enter a declaratory judgment determining its
obligations as to the Roadway. The court first expressed its concern about the implications of
determining that the Roadway is not a subdivision road, both for the public and for Teton County.
The court explained that such a determination could effectively require it to designate the Roadway
as a county road or subdivision road, a function which is normally performed by a county board
or highway district under Idaho Code section 40-202. The court ultimately declined to designate
the Roadway a subdivision road, stating:
                The record as it stands establishes only that the road consisted of access and
       utility easements which existed during the construction of the subdivision, and that
       during the time of the development of the subdivision, the Overlook was obligated
       to share pro-rata costs for the maintenance of the road during construction to
       prevent issues with traffic while the development was in the midst of construction.
       These facts are inadequate for the court to enter declaratory judgment defining the
       road at this juncture.
       The district court then ruled that the Overlook CC&amp;Rs do not create an obligation to
maintain the Roadway:
       There is no evidence established by the record that the road in question is within
       the subdivision and subject to the provisions obligating the HOA to maintain the
       road and ensure the road’s snow removal. . . . [T]he [c]ourt determines that there
       are no genuine issues of material fact precluding summary judgment and will
       GRANT summary judgment as to the HOA’s request for declaratory relief on this
       claim.
Further, the court pointed to its previous finding that Colton did not have privity of contract with
the Overlook HOA.




                                                 11
       Colton contends that the district court erred by ruling on summary judgment that the
Overlook CC&amp;Rs do not create an obligation to maintain the Roadway while also declining to
determine the designation of the Roadway. He insists that he submitted evidence establishing that
the disputed Roadway is located within the Overlook Subdivision’s boundaries, so there was a
genuine issue of material fact as to whether the Roadway is located within the Subdivision. He
argues that, by concluding that the Overlook CC&amp;Rs did not create a duty to maintain the
Roadway, the court also necessarily concluded that the Roadway is not located in the Overlook
Subdivision. Thus, he asserts that the district court contradicted itself. The individual Respondents
argue that Colton lacks standing to raise this issue because he is not a party to the Overlook
CC&amp;Rs. They further argue that, regardless of standing, the Roadway is an access and utility
easement, not a subdivision road, which is established by the plat boundary description and the
Development Agreement.
       First, we turn to the individual Respondents’ argument that Colton lacks standing to appeal
the court’s order of summary judgment. Under Idaho Appellate Rule 4, an aggrieved party has
standing to appeal an appealable judgment. An “aggrieved party” is defined “as any party
injuriously affected by the judgment.” First State Bank of Eldorado v. Rowe, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2622712"><a href="/opinion/2622712/first-state-bank-of-eldorado-v-rowe/#612" aria-description="Citation for case: First State Bank of Eldorado v. Rowe">142 Idaho 608, 612</a></span><pre class="inline">,
</pre><span class="citation" data-id="2622712"><a href="/opinion/2622712/first-state-bank-of-eldorado-v-rowe/#1150" aria-description="Citation for case: First State Bank of Eldorado v. Rowe">130 P.3d 1146, 1150</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2006) (quoting Fed. Land Bank of Spokane v. Parsons, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="1156690"><a href="/opinion/1156690/federal-land-bank-of-spokane-v-parsons/#547" aria-description="Citation for case: Federal Land Bank of Spokane v. Parsons">116 Idaho 545, 547</a></span><pre class="inline">,
</pre><span class="citation" data-id="1156690"><a href="/opinion/1156690/federal-land-bank-of-spokane-v-parsons/#1220" aria-description="Citation for case: Federal Land Bank of Spokane v. Parsons">777 P.2d 1218, 1220</a></span><pre class="inline"> (Ct. App. 1989)). Because Colton is a party to the individual Respondents’
counterclaim, he possesses an interest that may be affected by the judgment; therefore, he is
aggrieved and entitled to appeal. See Fed. Land Bank of Spokane, 116 Idaho at 547–48, 777 P.2d
at 1220–21. Additionally, as discussed above, Colton is also an aggrieved party because the adverse
declaratory judgment economically injures Colton. Because the district court concluded that the
Overlook HOA has no obligation to maintain the road, Colton must continue to pay the costs on
his own.
       This brings us to the issue of whether the district court erred in ruling that the Overlook
HOA has no obligation under the CC&amp;Rs to maintain the Roadway. While Colton argues that the
district court effectively contradicted itself by declining to legally designate the Roadway’s status
and then determining that the Roadway is not located within the Subdivision, these are actually
two separate rulings. The authority to designate a public highway rests with the county or highway
district commissioners under Idaho Code section 40-202, and any such designation must be
supported under section 40-208 by “substantial and competent evidence.” Flying A Ranch, Inc. v.



                                                 12
Cnty. Comm’rs of Fremont Cnty., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="3133268"><a href="/opinion/3133268/flying-a-ranch-v-county-commissioners-of-fremont-county/#942" aria-description="Citation for case: Flying &quot;A&quot; Ranch v. County Commissioners of Fremont County">157 Idaho 937, 942</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="3133268"><a href="/opinion/3133268/flying-a-ranch-v-county-commissioners-of-fremont-county/#654" aria-description="Citation for case: Flying &quot;A&quot; Ranch v. County Commissioners of Fremont County">342 P.3d 649, 654</a></span><pre class="inline"> (2015). The district court
concluded that the record before it was insufficient to designate the Roadway. That ruling is distinct
from the court’s separate finding that the Roadway was not located within the Overlook
Subdivision.
       Colton additionally argues that the record clearly shows that portions of the Roadway are
located within the Overlook Subdivision. Yet the district court concluded that there was no
evidence establishing this as a fact. “At the summary judgment stage, a trial court should refrain
from making such factual determinations until evidence is heard on the disputed facts and should
look to the affidavit itself to determine if it alleges facts which, if taken as true, would render the
evidence therein sufficient.” Watts v. Lynn, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9594308"><a href="/opinion/1350019/watts-v-lynn/#346" aria-description="Citation for case: Watts v. Lynn">125 Idaho 341, 346</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9594308"><a href="/opinion/1350019/watts-v-lynn/#1305" aria-description="Citation for case: Watts v. Lynn">870 P.2d 1300, 1305</a></span><pre class="inline"> (1994). As
the party moving for summary judgment on its counterclaim, the HOA had the burden “to prove
that no genuine issues of material fact exist and that it is entitled to judgment as a matter of law.”
Boise Mode, LLC v. Donahoe Pace &amp; Partners Ltd., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="880122"><a href="/opinion/880122/boise-mode-llc-v-donahoe-pace-partners-ltd/" aria-description="Citation for case: Boise Mode, LLC v. Donahoe Pace &amp; Partners Ltd.">154 Idaho 99</a></span><pre class="inline">, 103–04, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="880122"><a href="/opinion/880122/boise-mode-llc-v-donahoe-pace-partners-ltd/" aria-description="Citation for case: Boise Mode, LLC v. Donahoe Pace &amp; Partners Ltd.">294 P.3d 1111</a></span><pre class="inline">, 1115–
16 (2013). Only after this burden is met does the burden shift “to the nonmoving party to show the
existence of a genuine issue of material fact.” </pre><span class="citation" data-id="880122"><a href="/opinion/880122/boise-mode-llc-v-donahoe-pace-partners-ltd/#104" aria-description="Citation for case: Boise Mode, LLC v. Donahoe Pace &amp; Partners Ltd.">Id. at 104</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="880122"><a href="/opinion/880122/boise-mode-llc-v-donahoe-pace-partners-ltd/" aria-description="Citation for case: Boise Mode, LLC v. Donahoe Pace &amp; Partners Ltd.">294 P.3d at 1116</a></span><pre class="inline"> (quoting Asbury Park,
LLC v. Greenbriar Est. Homeowners’ Ass’n, Inc., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="874062"><a href="/opinion/874062/asbury-park-llc-v-greenbriar-estate-homeowners-assn/" aria-description="Citation for case: Asbury Park, LLC v. Greenbriar Estate Homeowners&#x27; Ass&#x27;n">152 Idaho 338</a></span><pre class="inline">, 343–44, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="874062"><a href="/opinion/874062/asbury-park-llc-v-greenbriar-estate-homeowners-assn/" aria-description="Citation for case: Asbury Park, LLC v. Greenbriar Estate Homeowners&#x27; Ass&#x27;n">271 P.3d 1194</a></span><pre class="inline">, 1199–
1200 (2012)).
       The CC&amp;Rs’ snow removal provision plainly obligates the HOA to snowplow roads within
the Subdivision. “Within” is defined as “being inside.” Within, Merriam-Webster’s Collegiate
Dictionary (11th ed. 2006). Thus, the dispositive issue in determining the HOA’s obligations under
the CC&amp;Rs for snowplowing the Roadway is whether the Roadway is located within or outside of
the Subdivision. It is worth noting that the HOA initially took the position in its memorandum in
support of its motion for summary judgment that the Roadway was a public county road and that
is why it did not fall within the roads to be maintained by the HOA. At the time of the hearing,
when the district court expressed concerns about the implications of declaring a road public,
particularly when Teton County had not been joined, the HOA shifted its position about the public
nature of the road. As the party moving for summary judgment on its own counterclaim, it was the
HOA’s burden to establish that the Roadway lies outside the Subdivision; yet it never made this
assertion below. Thus, the HOA could not carry its burden, and summary judgment should not
have been entered in its favor.




                                                  13
        The district court prematurely shifted the burden to Colton to establish the existence of a
genuine issue of material fact on this issue. While it determined that “[t]here is no evidence
established by the record that the road in question is within the subdivision,” it did not rule whether
the Roadway is located outside of the Subdivision’s boundaries, which was the HOA’s obligation
to prove on its motion for summary judgment on its counterclaim. Thus, the district court erred in
concluding that the HOA, as the moving party on its counterclaim, met its burden to prove that the
Roadway is located outside of the Subdivision. Our review of the record does not clearly establish
whether the Roadway lies within or outside the Subdivision. As noted, the only maps in the record
are inconclusive. See, e.g., Figure </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9604166"><a href="/opinion/1367562/idaho-branch-inc-of-associated-general-contractors-of-america-inc-v/" aria-description="Citation for case: Idaho Branch, Inc. of Associated General Contractors of...">1, supra.</a></span><pre class="inline"> Because that determination bears directly on the HOA’s
counterclaim and its potential obligations under the CC&amp;Rs, this uncertainty presents a genuine
issue of material fact.
        Simply put, the record contains insufficient evidence to establish the Roadway’s location
relative to the Subdivision. The Roadway’s true location—the primary issue of contention in this
case—remains an unresolved factual determination that was inappropriately dispensed with at the
summary judgment stage. As a result, the trial court’s treatments of Colton’s claims on the motion
to dismiss, and the HOA’s counterclaim on summary judgment, were inconsistent. Thus, we
reverse the district court’s grant of summary judgment in favor of the HOA and remand for
proceedings consistent with this opinion. On remand, the court must: (1) ascertain whether the
Roadway lies inside the boundaries of the Subdivision; (2) if it lies inside the boundaries,
determine the HOA’s and GNJ’s respective obligations under the CC&amp;Rs and the Development
Agreement; and (3) address Colton’s alternative claims, under both easement law and in equity,
and determine whether the property owners using the Roadway as their sole access route should
share in its maintenance costs. Because these are intensely factual determinations, we leave these
matters to the trial court.
C. We vacate the district court’s awards of attorney fees to the Respondents.
        Colton also appeals the district court’s two awards of attorney fees and costs totaling
$23,650 to the individual Respondents under Idaho Code sections 12-120(3) and 12-121. Since we
have reversed the district court’s orders (1) dismissing Colton’s claims against the individual
Respondents and (2) granting summary judgment in favor of the HOA, it is now unclear who will
ultimately prevail on remand; thus, we cannot review the award for an abuse of discretion.
Accordingly, we vacate the awards of attorney fees and costs.



                                                  14
D. Respondents are not entitled to attorney fees and costs on appeal under Idaho Code
   sections 12-120(3) or 12-121.
       Respondents have requested an award of attorney fees. However, attorney fees under Idaho
Code sections 12-120(3) and 12-121 may only be awarded to a prevailing party. Given our
disposition of this matter, Respondents are not prevailing parties and their request must be denied.
                                     IV.     CONCLUSION
          For the foregoing reasons, we reverse the district court’s order dismissing Colton’s claims
against Respondents and its order granting summary judgment in favor of Respondents on their
counterclaim. This matter is remanded to the district court for further proceedings consistent with
this opinion. We also vacate the district court’s award of attorney fees to Respondents under both
Idaho Code sections 12-120 and 12-121. Finally, we decline to award attorney fees on appeal to
any party. As the prevailing party on appeal, Colton is entitled to recover his costs as a matter of
right pursuant to Idaho Appellate Rule 40(a).
