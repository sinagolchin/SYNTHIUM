STITH, District Judge.


[¶1] Amyntas Hinckley (“Hinckley”) was employed by the University of Wyoming
(“University”) as a marketing and communications specialist. According to her amended
complaint, she was subjected to discrimination based on sex, was paid a lower salary than
a male counterpart for equivalent work, and endured a hostile environment, retaliation,
fraud, and intentional infliction of emotional distress. The University filed a motion to
dismiss her complaint, claiming immunity from tort liability under the Wyoming
Governmental Claims Act, failure to exhaust administrative remedies, and failure to state
a claim upon which relief could be granted. The district court granted the University’s
motion to dismiss and Hinckley appealed. We affirm.

                                        ISSUES

[¶2] Appellant Hinckley raises five issues and Appellee University raises six issues in
response, which this court rephrases as follows:

      1) Whether the technical deficiencies in Appellant’s brief warrant summary
         affirmance of the district court;

      2) Whether the Wyoming Governmental Claims Act bars Hinckley’s claims; and

      3) Whether the Wyoming Fair Employment Practices Act limits the district court’s
         jurisdiction to reviewing the outcome of an administrative fair hearing under
         </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 27-9-106</span><pre class="inline">(k) and the Wyoming Administrative Procedure Act;

                                        FACTS

[¶3] According to her amended complaint, Hinckley was employed by the University as
a marketing and communications specialist. She alleged that she was subject to
discriminatory treatment during her employment, a male peer was paid a higher salary for
equivalent work and given preferential treatment, and her supervisor otherwise fostered an
environment of unequal treatment. After she raised concerns concerning unequal
treatment, the amended complaint alleges that the University retaliated against her by
issuing a warning letter falsely claiming performance deficiencies and denied her the
opportunity to disprove the claims. The University terminated Hinckley’s employment.
Post-termination, the amended complaint alleges that the University provided false
information to the Wyoming Department of Workforce Services, falsely claiming that she
had left work early on one day, which delayed her receipt of unemployment compensation
benefits.

monetary damages and injunctive relief for reinstatement and to compel the University to
implement workplace changes. The University filed a motion to dismiss and a notice of

</pre><span class="citation no-link">Id.</span><pre class="inline"> (citing Sheesley, ¶ 34, 573 P.3d at 547). When reviewing a motion to dismiss, we
examine the same materials and apply the same standards as the district court. Sheesley,

                                       DISCUSSION

   I.     The Technical Deficiencies of Appellant’s Notice of Appeal and Brief.

[¶12] Appellee University points to several technical deficiencies in Hinckley’s notice of
appeal and brief. They include: (1) the notice of appeal identifies two specific orders being
appealed but also generically lists “all interlocutory orders;” (2) Hinckley has failed to
designate a record on appeal; (3) Hinckley fails to support her brief with cogent argument
and relevant authority; (4) Hinckley’s brief fails to comply with W.R.A.P. 7.01 in multiple
ways, including failure to have a statement of jurisdiction and an appendix.

[¶13] “A pro se litigant is entitled to some leniency from the stringent standards applied
to formal pleadings drafted by attorneys. However, there must be a reasonable adherence
to the procedural rules and requirements of the court.” Byrnes v. Harper, </pre><span class="citation no-link">2019 WY 20</span><pre class="inline">, ¶ 3,
</pre><span class="citation" data-id="6535639"><a href="/opinion/6658269/byrnes-v-harper/#366" aria-description="Citation for case: Byrnes v. Harper">435 P.3d 364, 366</a></span><pre class="inline"> (Wyo. 2019) (quoting Hodgins v. State, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2552516"><a href="/opinion/2552516/hodgins-v-state/#1262" aria-description="Citation for case: Hodgins v. State">1 P.3d 1259, 1262</a></span><pre class="inline"> (Wyo. 2000)).
“This Court will impose sanctions including, but not limited to, summary affirmance,
pursuant to W.R.A.P. 1.03 on pro se litigants who fail to comply with these rules.” </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2552516"><a href="/opinion/2552516/hodgins-v-state/" aria-description="Citation for case: Hodgins v. State">Id.</a></span><pre class="inline"> In
addition, “It is well established that the appellant bears the responsibility of bringing forth
a sufficient record for the Court’s review.” O’Donoghue v. Jim Vito Constr., Inc., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="11325638"><a href="/opinion/10858237/victorine-odonoghue-v-jim-vito-construction-inc-and-jim-vito/#12" aria-description="Citation for case: Victorine O&#x27;Donoghue v. Jim Vito Construction, Inc., and...">2026 WY
53, ¶ 12</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation no-link">589 P.3d 342</span><pre class="inline">, 346 (Wyo. 2026) (citation omitted).

[¶14] Rule 1.03(a) of the Wyoming Rules of Appellate Procedure (W.R.A.P.) explicitly
provides “[t]he timely filing of a notice of appeal, which complies with Rule 2.07(a), is
jurisdictional.” Am. Collection Sys., Inc., ¶ 7, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="10034377"><a href="/opinion/9567764/american-collection-systems-inc-a-wyoming-corporation-v-lacy-d-judkins/#554" aria-description="Citation for case: American Collection Systems, Inc., a Wyoming Corporation...">550 P.3d at 554</a></span><pre class="inline">. To comply with W.R.A.P
2.07(a)(2), a party’s notice of appeal must “identify the judgment or appealable order being
appealed.” </pre><span class="citation" data-id="10034377"><a href="/opinion/9567764/american-collection-systems-inc-a-wyoming-corporation-v-lacy-d-judkins/" aria-description="Citation for case: American Collection Systems, Inc., a Wyoming Corporation...">Id.</a></span><pre class="inline"> (citation omitted). “A notice of appeal ‘only perfects an appeal of the
order(s) identified in the notice.’” </pre><span class="citation" data-id="10034377"><a href="/opinion/9567764/american-collection-systems-inc-a-wyoming-corporation-v-lacy-d-judkins/" aria-description="Citation for case: American Collection Systems, Inc., a Wyoming Corporation...">Id.</a></span><pre class="inline"> (quoting EOG Res. Inc., v. JJLM Land, LLC, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9350791"><a href="/opinion/9355319/eog-resources-inc-a-delaware-corporation-v-jjlm-land-llc-a-wyoming/#41" aria-description="Citation for case: Eog Resources, Inc., a Delaware Corporation v. Jjlm Land,...">2022
WY 162, ¶ 41</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9350791"><a href="/opinion/9355319/eog-resources-inc-a-delaware-corporation-v-jjlm-land-llc-a-wyoming/#616" aria-description="Citation for case: Eog Resources, Inc., a Delaware Corporation v. Jjlm Land,...">522 P.3d 605, 616</a></span><pre class="inline"> (Wyo. 2022)). If an order is not properly identified in
the notice of appeal, this Court is “without jurisdiction” to review it. </pre><span class="citation" data-id="9350791"><a href="/opinion/9355319/eog-resources-inc-a-delaware-corporation-v-jjlm-land-llc-a-wyoming/" aria-description="Citation for case: Eog Resources, Inc., a Delaware Corporation v. Jjlm Land,...">Id.</a></span><pre class="inline">

[¶15] Hinckley did identify two specific orders in her Notice of Appeal, the “October 16,
2025 Order of Dismissal Without Prejudice” and the “November 25, 2025 Order Following
Reconsideration Hearing.” Hinckley’s Notice of Appeal adequately identifies those two
orders. This Court does not have jurisdiction over any other orders in this appeal, which
are identified only generically, and we will not consider them.

[¶16] Hinckley’s failure to designate a record on appeal would be a sufficient reason for
summary affirmance, but the University did designate a record and the materials necessary
to review the dismissal order are adequately before the Court. With respect to lack of



                                              5
citation to authority or cogent argument, Hinckley’s brief is not a model of clarity, but the
issues she identifies and the gravamen of her argument are ascertainable.

[¶17] In summary, although the technical deficiencies of Hinckley’s notice and brief
would permit summary affirmance, the Court will not impose sanctions on Hinckley and
exercises its discretion to consider her appeal on the merits.

   II.    The Wyoming Governmental Claims Act Bars Hinckley’s Tort Claims for
          Money Damages But Does Not Bar Her Claims for Injunctive Relief.

[¶18] The University argues that the Wyoming Governmental Claims Act (“WGCA”) bars
all of Hinckley’s tort claims, which are Hinckley’s causes of action for wrongful
termination in violation of Wyoming public policy, fraud and intentional infliction of
emotional distress. As discussed below, the WGCA bars Hinckley’s tort claims for money
damages, but does not bar Hinckley’s claim for wrongful termination to the extent that
Hinckley seeks injunctive relief.

[¶19] The University was created by the Wyoming Constitution and is a governmental
entity within the meaning of the Wyoming Governmental Claim Act. </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 1</span><pre class="inline">-
39-101 et seq.; Wyo. Const. art. 7, § 15; City of Laramie, ¶ 28, 542 P.3d at 617. </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat.
Ann. § 1-39-104</span><pre class="inline">(a) provides: “[a] governmental entity and its public employees while
acting within the scope of duties are granted immunity from liability for any tort except as
provided by W.S. 1-39-105 through 1-39-112.” “When it adopted the WGCA, the
Wyoming Legislature balanced the equities between the ‘inherently unfair and inequitable
results which occur in the strict application of the doctrine of governmental immunity’ and
the need to provide governmental services while protecting the public coffers.” Whitham
v. Feller, </pre><span class="citation no-link">2018 WY 43</span><pre class="inline">, ¶ 24, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="6533645"><a href="/opinion/6656373/whitham-v-feller/#1269" aria-description="Citation for case: Whitham v. Feller">415 P.3d 1264, 1269</a></span><pre class="inline"> (Wyo. 2018) (quoting </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann.
§ 1-39-102</span><pre class="inline">(a)).

 [¶20] Fraud and intentional infliction of emotional distress are not exceptions to immunity
listed in the WGCA. </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. §§ 1-39-105</span><pre class="inline"> to 1-39-112. Hinckley seeks monetary
damages only for those claims and the WGCA grants the University immunity barring
Hinckley from pursuing those claims. Hinckley’s amended complaint also includes a claim
for wrongful termination in violation of Wyoming’s public policy, which would be a tort
and for which there is no enumerated exception under the WGCA. To the extent that
Hinckley seeks monetary damages for wrongful termination, the WGCA also bars that
claim.

[¶21] The WGCA does not, however, prevent suits for injunction against a governmental
entity. </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 1-39-116</span><pre class="inline"> (“Nothing in this section prohibits any proceedings for
mandamus, prohibition, habeas corpus, injunction or quo warranto”). With respect to her
claim for wrongful termination, Hinckley seeks not just financial compensation, but also



                                             6
to be reinstated to her position and to compel the University to implement certain
workplace changes, which are forms of injunctive relief.

[¶22] Injunctions are governed by </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 1-28-101</span><pre class="inline"> et seq. </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann.
§ 1-28-102</span><pre class="inline"> provides:

       When it appears by the petition that the plaintiff is entitled to relief consisting of
       restraining the commission or continuance of some act the commission or
       continuance of which during the litigation would produce great or irreparable injury
       to the plaintiff, or when during the litigation it appears that the defendant is doing,
       threatens to do, or is procuring to be done some act in violation of the plaintiff&#x27;s
       rights respecting the subject of the action and tending to render the judgment
       ineffectual, a temporary order may be granted restraining the act. The order may
       also be granted in any case where it is specially authorized by statute and by
       municipal ordinance adopted pursuant to W.S. 15-1-103(a)(xlvi).

</pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 1-28-102</span><pre class="inline">; Degenfelder v. Wyoming Educ. Ass’n, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="11328076"><a href="/opinion/10860669/nicolette-and-travis-leck-and-victoria-haight-v-wyoming-education/#19" aria-description="Citation for case: Nicolette and Travis Leck and Victoria Haight v. Wyoming...">2026 WY 54, ¶ 19</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation no-link">589
P.3d 346</span><pre class="inline">, 353 (Wyo. 2026) (“An injunction, whether preliminary or permanent, is
authorized by statute”). The WGCA does not bar Hinckley’s claim for wrongful
termination to the extent that she seeks injunctive relief. As discussed below, however,
Hinckley’s request for injunctive relief as a remedy for her claim for wrongful termination
in violation of Wyoming public policy is barred for different reasons.

   III.   Hinckley’s Cause of Action for Wrongful Termination in Violation of Public
          Policy Is Not a Cognizable Tort Because There Is an Adequate Statutory
          Remedy for the Wrong Alleged.

[¶23] Hinckley’s claim for wrongful termination in violation of public policy fails because
there is an adequate statutory remedy for the wrong alleged. Hinckley’s amended
complaint alleges that Hinckley “was subjected to discriminatory treatment during her
employment in violation of Wyoming’s anti-discrimination statutes.” The factual
allegations focus on discrimination based on sex, including that a male counterpart was
paid a higher salary, that she was reprimanded for highlighting a controversial female
speaker in social media posts, that her supervisor expressed reluctance to promote women
in business, that she was questioned about why the Women in Business Club was featured
in a video, that she was instructed not to promote events organized around Women In
Business, and that her employer fostered an environment of unequal treatment and
retaliated against her for raising concerns about unequal treatment. Hinckley’s claim for
wrongful termination in violation of public policy states: “Defendant’s termination of
Plaintiff violated Wyoming public policy, including protections against retaliation for
raising workplace grievances.”




                                              7
[¶24] “In order to prevail on a claim of wrongful termination against public policy, a
plaintiff must satisfy two requirements: 1) the termination violated a well-established
public policy; and 2) no other remedy is available to protect the interests of the terminated
employee or of society.” McLean v. Hyland Enters., Inc., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2631891"><a href="/opinion/2631891/mclean-v-hyland-enterprises-inc/#24" aria-description="Citation for case: McLean v. Hyland Enterprises, Inc.">2001 WY 111, ¶ 24</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2631891"><a href="/opinion/2631891/mclean-v-hyland-enterprises-inc/#1268" aria-description="Citation for case: McLean v. Hyland Enterprises, Inc.">34 P.3d
1262, 1268</a></span><pre class="inline"> (Wyo. 2001); McGarvey v. Key Prop. Mgmt. LLC, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2562004"><a href="/opinion/2562004/mcgarvey-v-key-property-management-llc/#13" aria-description="Citation for case: McGarvey v. KEY PROPERTY MANAGEMENT LLC">2009 WY 84, ¶ 13</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2562004"><a href="/opinion/2562004/mcgarvey-v-key-property-management-llc/#507" aria-description="Citation for case: McGarvey v. KEY PROPERTY MANAGEMENT LLC">211 P.3d
503, 507</a></span><pre class="inline"> (Wyo. 2009) (same). In the present case, Hinckley cannot satisfy the second
element, namely, the lack of other remedy. Allen v. Safeway Stores Inc., </pre><span class="citation" data-id="1174712"><a href="/opinion/1174712/allen-v-safeway-stores-inc/#284" aria-description="Citation for case: Allen v. Safeway Stores, Inc.">699 P.2d 277, 284</a></span><pre class="inline">
(Wyo.1985) (both Wyoming Fair Employment Practices Act and 42 U.S.C. § 2000e et
seq. provide remedies for termination based on sex discrimination).

[¶25] The Wyoming Fair Employment Practices Act (“WFEPA”) prohibits the same
conduct for which Hinckley seeks a remedy in her amended complaint. </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann.
§ 27-9-105</span><pre class="inline">(a) provides:

       It is a discriminatory or unfair employment practice:

              (i)    For an employer to refuse to hire, to discharge, to promote or demote,
                     or to discriminate in matters of compensation or the terms, conditions
                     or privileges of employment against, a qualified disabled person or
                     any person otherwise qualified, because of age, sex, race, creed, color,
                     national origin, ancestry or pregnancy.

</pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 27-9-105</span><pre class="inline">(a).

[¶26] The central claim of Hinckley’s complaint is discrimination in compensation, terms
and conditions of her employment based on sex and falls squarely within the scope of </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo.
Stat. Ann. § 27-9-105</span><pre class="inline">(a).

[¶27] </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 27-9-106</span><pre class="inline"> provides a statutory remedy for a violation of </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat.
Ann. § 27-9-105</span><pre class="inline">(a) and also provides a process that a complainant must follow to pursue
that remedy. The available relief under </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 27-9-106</span><pre class="inline">(n) includes
reinstatement and requiring the employer to cease and desist from the discriminatory or
unfair practice, similar to the injunctive remedy that Hinckley seeks in her amended
complaint. McLean, ¶ 38, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="2631891"><a href="/opinion/2631891/mclean-v-hyland-enterprises-inc/#1271" aria-description="Citation for case: McLean v. Hyland Enterprises, Inc.">34 P.3d at 1271</a></span><pre class="inline"> (“nothing requires the administrative remedy to
be equivalent to a common law claim”).

[¶28] The administrative process is set out in </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 27-9-106</span><pre class="inline">(a). Subsection
(a) states that “any person claiming to be aggrieved by a discriminatory or unfair
employment practice may, personally or through his attorney, make, sign and file with the
department [of workforce services] within six (6) months of the alleged violation a verified,
written complaint . . .” </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 27-9-106</span><pre class="inline">(a). Upon the filing of a complaint,
“[t]he department [of workforce services] shall investigate to determine the validity of the
charges and issue a determination thereupon.” </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 27-9-106</span><pre class="inline">(a). If the


                                             8
employee or employer “is aggrieved by the department’s determination, the aggrieved
party may request a fair hearing.” </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 27-9-106</span><pre class="inline">(k).

[¶29] The Wyoming Department of Workforce Services, acting through its Labor
Standards Division, has promulgated rules governing such a fair hearing. See Labor
Standards, ch. 3, Fair Employment Rules (hereinafter “Rules”). Under the Rules, “[i]f
Labor Standards determines that probable cause does exist to conclude that discrimination
has occurred, Labor Standards shall endeavor to make whole the complainant through its
conciliation process.” Rules, § 4(h). If no settlement is reached within forty-five days after
both parties receive the probable cause determination, Labor Standards shall make a
determination that the conciliation process has failed and, upon request of either party, refer
the complaint to an independent hearing officer for a fair hearing. Id. Fair hearings are
conducted by an independent hearing officer. Rules, § 5(a).

[¶30] Judicial review of a hearing officer’s decision is provided by the Rules as follows:
“Pursuant to W.S. § 16-3-114, any complainant or respondent aggrieved or adversely
affected by the hearing officer’s decision may file a petition for judicial review. Petitions
for judicial review of the hearing officer’s decision shall be made pursuant to Rule 12 of
the Wyoming Rules of Appellate Procedure.” Rules, § 6(a).

[¶31] W.R.A.P. 12.01 provides:

       To the extent judicial review of administrative action by a district court is available,
       any person aggrieved or adversely affected in fact by a final decision of an agency
       in a contested case, or who is aggrieved or adversely affected in fact by any other
       agency action or inaction . . . may obtain such review as provided in this rule.

W.R.A.P. 12.01.

[¶32] W.R.A.P. 12.03 provides:

       The proceedings for judicial review under Rule 12 shall be instituted by filing a
       petition for review in the district court[.]

W.R.A.P. 12.03

[¶33] The public-policy exception exists to vindicate important social policies that would
otherwise go unremedied when an employee is discharged. Where a statutory or other
remedy already addresses the policy violation that led to the discharge, a separate common-
law tort claim is unnecessary. Allen, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="1174712"><a href="/opinion/1174712/allen-v-safeway-stores-inc/#284" aria-description="Citation for case: Allen v. Safeway Stores, Inc.">699 P.2d at 284</a></span><pre class="inline">. Hinckley has failed to show that
there is no other remedy and her claim for wrongful termination in violation of public
policy fails.



                                              9
    IV.     Hinckley’s Causes of Action for “Discrimination under Wyoming Law” and
            “Hostile Work Environment” Fail Because They Were Not Filed As a
            Petition for Review of Any Agency Action.

[¶34] To the extent that Hinckley’s causes of action for “Discrimination under Wyoming
Law” and “Hostile Work Environment” are based upon WFEPA, those claims also fail.
The amended complaint does not disclose whether Hinckley filed a complaint with the
department of workforce services, whether an investigation was conducted or whether a
fair hearing was requested or held. What is clear from the record, however, is that the
amended complaint is not a “petition for judicial review” of agency action.

[¶35] “Generally, where an agency prescribes an administrative process for resolving
disputes, the parties must exhaust those administrative remedies before seeking court
intervention.” Sorensen v. Halling, </pre><span class="citation multiple-matches"><a href="/c/WY/2025/8/">2025 WY 8</a></span><pre class="inline">, ¶ 10, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="10779980"><a href="/opinion/10313392/l-michael-sorensen-and-sheri-m-sorensen-individually-and-as-trustees-of/#1245" aria-description="Citation for case: L. Michael Sorensen and Sheri M. Sorensen, Individually...">561 P.3d 1241, 1245</a></span><pre class="inline"> (Wyo. 2025).
“A district court is vested with the discretion to decline jurisdiction over a matter because
a party failed to exhaust its administrative remedies.” Thomas Gilcrease Found. for
Gilcrease Hoback One Charitable Tr. v. Cavallaro, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="4175205"><a href="/opinion/4397952/thomas-gilcrease-foundation-as-trustee-for-the-gilcrease-hoback-one/#10" aria-description="Citation for case: Thomas Gilcrease Foundation, as Trustee for the Gilcrease...">2017 WY 67, ¶ 10</a></span><pre class="inline">, </pre><span class="citation" data-id="4175205"><a href="/opinion/4397952/thomas-gilcrease-foundation-as-trustee-for-the-gilcrease-hoback-one/#169" aria-description="Citation for case: Thomas Gilcrease Foundation, as Trustee for the Gilcrease...">397 P.3d 166, 169</a></span><pre class="inline">
(Wyo. 2017). Under WFEPA, judicial review of a fair-hearing decision is obtained by
filing a petition for review in the district court pursuant to W.R.A.P. 12.03 and the Wyoming
Administrative Procedure Act. See </pre><span class="citation no-link">Wyo. Stat. Ann. § 16-3-114</span><pre class="inline">; W.R.A.P. 12.01, 12.03.
Hinckley did not file a petition for review. She commenced the action by filing a complaint
under W.R.C.P. 3, which is the ordinary pleading used to initiate an original civil action.
Because the amended complaint was not a petition for review of any agency action, the
district court properly exercised its discretion to decline jurisdiction over the WFEPA
claims. 2

                                    CONCLUSION

[¶37] The WGCA bars Hinckley’s tort claims for fraud and intentional infliction of
emotional distress, for which she seeks only monetary damages. The WGCA also bars her
claim for wrongful termination in violation of Wyoming public policy to the extent that she
seeks monetary damages. Hinckley’s claim for wrongful termination, to the extent that it
seeks injunctive relief, and her claims for discrimination under Wyoming law and hostile
environment, fail because they are all based on alleged sex discrimination for which there
is an adequate statutory remedy under WFEPA that requires any district court action to be
brought as a petition for review under W.R.A.P. 12.03 rather than as a complaint under
W.R.C.P. 3.

[¶38] Affirmed.
